The "Logic & Debate" Mindset

At the higher score bands, IELTS Writing is no longer purely a language test, but rather a "logic and debate exam." Many native English-speaking university students who take the test without preparation often only score a 6.5. This is because they think, "My English is so good, I can just write whatever." As a result, due to a lack of rigorous logic or going off-topic, their writing completely fails to meet the official IELTS requirements.

Current total score: Listening 7 + Reading 7 + Writing 6 + Speaking 6 = 26 points Average: 6.5

Listening

Key to 7.5 - 8

Candidates scoring a 7 typically do not lose points in Part 1 and Part 2; their main stumbling blocks are Part 3 (multiple-choice for long conversations) and Part 4 (academic lecture fill-in-the-blanks).

He no longer needs extensive general listening. It is recommended that he specifically tackles Part 3 multiple-choice questions daily to build his anti-interference ability to "read long options while listening." Simultaneously, he should compile common synonym replacements for Part 4 fill-in-the-blank questions.

Reading

Key to 7.5 - 8

Scoring a 7 in Reading indicates that vocabulary and the parsing of complex sentences are absolutely not an issue. Any further point loss is often because the candidate gets tangled in the convoluted logic of True/False/Not Given questions, or misses the mark when summarizing paragraph main ideas (Heading questions).

Have him isolate these two specific question types from his past mistakes. He shouldn't focus on doing more new practice tests, but rather on purely understanding what the "trap logic" set by the examiners actually is.

Writing Mastery

- Introduce "High-Quality Grading" (Most Important): Instead of watching videos and comparing essays on his own, it is much better to find a reliable IELTS teacher or use high-quality AI grading tools for detailed corrections of his essays. An external perspective is necessary to point out logical gaps and unnatural word choices.
- Deep Revision > Frantic Writing: Do not blindly rush to write a massive number of essays just to feel productive. The correct approach is to write 2 essays a week, but each one must go through a closed loop: "write -> get it graded -> summarize mistakes -> rewrite," until the logic and vocabulary of that specific essay reach the 7-band standard.
- The core of a Band 7 lies in Task 2's Task Response: (whether it perfectly addresses the prompt and has depth in argumentation) and Coherence & Cohesion (whether the argument flows smoothly). This requires extensive outlining practice, not just padding the word count.
- A Persian native language background might present a few typical "hidden bottlenecks" in writing:
1) Invisible point loss from "Articles and Prepositions": Persian does not have a strict and high-frequency definite/indefinite article system like "a / an / the" in English. Many native Persian speakers, even those with advanced vocabulary and complex sentence structures, frequently omit "the" or misuse prepositions. IELTS examiners hold a very high standard for Grammatical Range and Accuracy (GRA) when awarding a 7. If the essay is riddled with these minor flaws, the score can easily get locked at 6.5.
2) Expressions that are "Too Ornate but Lack Direct Logic": Persian is a beautiful, highly poetic language. This often leads Iranian students to habitually write very long, complex, and sometimes roundabout sentences in English Task 2 essays. However, the underlying logic of IELTS Academic Writing (Task 2) is extremely direct and "utilitarian"—examiners want to see straightforward arguments and rigorous logical deduction. Sacrificing clarity for the sake of fancy vocabulary and complex syntax will backfire.

Speaking Survival Kit

He can collect common topics, predict subjects for conversation practice, and improve reaction speed.

1 Overcoming a Blank Mind

"Legally" Lie and Abandon Depth

In IELTS Speaking, "truth" is worthless; being "fluent" is king. When encountering an unexpected question, do not hesitate—pick the simplest words and just "make it up."

(e.g., "No way! It sounds super boring to me. I'm an energetic person and I prefer working outdoors or interacting with people rather than standing next to old objects all day.")

2 Buy Thinking Time

Use Advanced "Nonsense" (Fillers)

Memorize a few sets of native-like "buffering sentences" (Fillers).

(e.g., "Well, to be honest, I've never really thought about it before, but off the top of my head I'd say..."

or "That's quite an interesting question. If I had to choose, I suppose...")

3 Surviving 2-Min Torture

The Timeline (PPF) Expansion

Apply the Past - Present - Future (PPF) timeline model. Take "favorite actor" as an example:

  • - Past: I first watched his movie a few years ago, and back then I thought it was just okay...
  • - Present: But his recent work completely changed my perspective, his acting is...
  • - Future: I heard he is going to direct a new movie next year, and I will definitely go to the cinema to support him.

Once the timeline is stretched out, two minutes won't even be enough time for him to finish talking.
