Pure Dry-Lab Researcher (In Silico) vs. Wet-Lab / Quality Control (QC)
Core Career Pivot
Conducting R&D entirely through computer code, AI models, and biological big data—without touching pipettes or animal experiments.

[Role Scope] Pure dry-lab (in silico) researchers write Python/PyTorch code daily on Linux/GPU servers, invoking protein or chemical language models for million-scale virtual screening, vaccine epitope prediction, mRNA sequence optimization, or clinical multi-omics drug-resistance mining. By contrast, wet-lab researchers culture cells, run gels, and perform in vivo mouse validation, while Quality Control (QC) staff test batch drug compliance on factory floors following fixed SOPs. Most traditional pharmacy graduates crowd into wet-lab or QC roles, whereas an interdisciplinary profile combining a "Pharmacology background + pure dry-lab AI" represents an exceptionally scarce, high-compensation talent tier in South Korea.
Lead Optimization & Developability Scientist
Core Pharma R&D Role
Transforming an initial target-binding molecule or peptide into a genuinely safe, stable, non-toxic clinical drug candidate for humans.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details

Pre-trained Biological & Chemical Language Models (PLM / RLM / CLM: ESM-2, ProtT5, RiNALMo, RNA-FM, ChemBERTa)
Core AI Foundation
Large foundation models that treat amino acid sequences, RNA sequences, or chemical formulas as "foreign-language text" to automatically extract deep evolutionary and structural patterns.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
Without requiring costly 3D crystal structures, pre-trained models perform self-supervised learning across tens of millions of proteins (e.g., Meta's ESM-2/ESM3, ProtT5), RNAs (e.g., RNA-FM, RiNALMo, CodonBERT), or small-molecule SMILES strings (e.g., ChemBERTa, Mole-BERT), converting any peptide or molecule into a dense numerical vector (Embedding, such as 480-dim or 768-dim) rich in physicochemical and spatial-contact information.
Frozen Feature Extraction (Frozen Embeddings) vs. Full Fine-Tuning & LoRA
Beginner-Friendly Equalizer
Locking a foundation model's original weights and using it purely as an "advanced feature extractor" lets you run top-journal baselines on free Google Colab in minutes.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
On small biomedical datasets of only a few thousand samples, modifying every parameter of a massive foundation model (Full Fine-Tuning) not only demands expensive GPU compute but also easily collapses from severe overfitting. The Frozen (Freeze) strategy keeps pre-trained weights fixed, directly extracts sequence vectors (applying Mean Pooling while masking out Padding Tokens to turn variable-length sequences into fixed-length vectors), and feeds them into lightweight LightGBM tree models or MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) classifiers; meanwhile, LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) trains only a tiny fraction of bypass adapter parameters for lightweight fine-tuning.
Anti-Leakage Data Splits: Homology Cluster Split & Bemis–Murcko Scaffold Split
#1 Code Review Red Line
Rejecting self-deceptive Random Splits by forcing the test set to contain only unseen sequence families or novel chemical core scaffolds.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
Under a naive random split, highly similar homologous peptides or "twin molecules" differing by just a single methyl group appear in both the training and test sets, artificially inflating accuracy (Data Leakage); likewise, performing global feature normalization (Scaling) prior to splitting leaks the test-set distribution. Rigorous practice requires: clustering peptides/epitopes by sequence similarity or holding out unseen MHC alleles (Held-out MHC); and for small molecules, calling RDKit to extract Bemis–Murcko ring scaffolds (Scaffold Split) so the test set consists entirely of novel chemical cores, genuinely testing generalization.
Imbalanced Biomedical Metrics: AUROC, AUPRC (Area Under Precision-Recall Curve), MCC (Matthews Correlation Coefficient), RMSE
Rigorous Statistical Evaluation
In real-world drug discovery datasets where "only 2 out of 100 molecules are active," AUPRC and MCC reveal a model's true discriminative power.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
Biological screening datasets typically exhibit extreme positive-to-negative class imbalance. Under such skew, plain accuracy or AUROC can easily appear deceptively high while top-selected candidates are flooded with false positives. AUPRC specifically measures hit purity (precision) and recall among high-confidence predictions, while MCC (ranging from -1 to +1) synthesizes all four cells of the confusion matrix (true/false positives and true/false negatives)—making it the hardcore classification metric most valued by top Korean bioinformatics journals; RMSE (Root Mean Square Error) is used for continuous numerical regression tasks (such as clearance prediction).
Interpretability & High-Dimensional Statistics: SHAP Attribution, LASSO Regularization, Curse of Dimensionality & FDR
Pharmacological Bridge
Opening the AI black box to pinpoint exactly which amino acid physicochemical property, molecular substructure, or gene determines efficacy and toxicity.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
① Grounded in game theory, SHAP quantifies each input feature's positive or negative contribution to an individual prediction, enabling you to explain via membrane pharmacology or metabolic chemistry why a model flags a specific peptide as hemolytic. ② Faced with clinical datasets spanning tens of thousands of gene/immune features across only ~100 patients (the Curse of Dimensionality), ordinary regression inevitably overfits; LASSO applies an L1 penalty term to shrink irrelevant feature coefficients strictly to zero for automated feature selection. ③ FDR (False Discovery Rate correction) stringently controls false positives across tens of thousands of simultaneous gene hypothesis tests.
Frontier Generative Models & Toolchain: Flow Matching / Diffusion / GNN / FASTA / SMILES / RDKit / PyTDC
Dry-Lab Engineering Stack
The modern computational pharmacology toolkit advancing from "scoring existing molecules" to "de novo design of novel antibodies and small molecules on demand."
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
FASTA and SMILES are standard plain-text representations for biological sequences and chemical molecules, respectively; RDKit and Morgan Fingerprints handle chemical structure standardization (Canonicalization) and substructure computation; PyTDC (Therapeutics Data Commons) is Harvard's open-source standardized drug discovery database; Graph Neural Networks (GNN / Graph Contrastive Learning GCL / Equivariant GNNs) treat molecules as graphs composed of atoms and chemical bonds; and Diffusion models alongside Flow Matching (such as SE(3) Flow Matching) generate novel antibodies (e.g., TiDE-Ab) or anticancer small molecules (e.g., G2D-Diff) directly in 3D space or latent space conditioned on pharmacological profiles.
Epitope, pMHC Presentation, Neoantigen & Antibody Fc Effector Functions (ADCC/ADCP)
Computational Immunology & Vaccines
Two computational levers in vaccines and immunotherapy: selecting short antigenic peptides upfront to prime T/B cells, and optimizing antibody clearance functions downstream.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
An Epitope is the key short peptide fragment on a pathogen or tumor protein (tumor-specific mutant epitopes are termed Neoantigens) recognized by the immune system, which must bind and be presented by Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC-I/II) molecules (pMHC) to activate T cells. In antibody-mediated protection, beyond viral neutralization via the head Fab domain, binding of the tail Fc domain to immune-cell receptors to trigger Antibody-Dependent Cellular Cytotoxicity (ADCC) and Phagocytosis (ADCP) is equally critical—Systems Serology uses machine learning to integrate multidimensional antibody subclasses (e.g., IgG3) and glycosylation features to map vaccine protective immunity holistically.
Therapeutic Index / Safety Gating, Erythrocyte Hemolysis & Allergenicity
Peptide Safety Red Line
High antimicrobial or immunogenic potency alone is not enough—dangerous peptides that puncture human red blood cell membranes or trigger allergic reactions must be filtered out during in silico screening.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
Many potent antimicrobial or cationic peptides carry excessive positive net charge and contiguous hydrophobic amino acids, causing them to insert readily into human erythrocyte phospholipid bilayers and induce membrane pore formation and rupture (Hemolysis). Jointly modeling "immunogenicity / antimicrobial activity (e.g., Minimum Inhibitory Concentration, MIC)" alongside "hemolysis / cytotoxicity / allergenicity" in code to delineate a 2D high-activity, low-toxicity screening zone constitutes computational pharmacology's "Therapeutic Window safety gating."
ADMET, Hepatic Microsomal Stability (HLM/MLM), CYP450 Metabolic Soft Spots & hERG Cardiotoxicity
PK/Toxicology Core Expertise
Core pharmacokinetic and toxicological parameters determining whether a new drug candidate survives in animals and humans without causing liver or cardiac injury.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
ADMET stands for Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, and Toxicity. Hepatic Cytochrome P450 (CYP450) enzymes are the primary machinery breaking down xenobiotic drugs, and the vulnerable chemical bonds on a molecule most susceptible to CYP450 oxidation, dealkylation, or hydrolysis are called "Metabolic Soft Spots." Because mouse (MLM) and human liver microsomes (HLM) exhibit marked interspecies differences in CYP450 isoform composition and clearance kinetics—and translating in vitro assays to real in vivo exposure is heavily shaped by plasma protein binding—cross-species and in vitro-to-in vivo transfer modeling is exceptionally valuable; meanwhile, hERG is a critical cardiac potassium channel toxicity target that must be avoided to prevent arrhythmia.
mRNA Vaccine Sequence Engineering: 5'/3' UTR, CDS, CAI, GC%, Minimum Free Energy (MFE), DegScore & m1Ψ Modification
Nucleic Acid Vaccine Loop
The exact same antigen protein can be encoded by countless mRNA sequences; tuning codons, UTRs, and folding structure multiplies in vivo protein yield and stability.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
An mRNA construct consists of the antigen-encoding Coding Sequence (CDS) flanked by 5' and 3' Untranslated Regions (5' UTR and 3' UTR) that regulate ribosome binding and mRNA half-life. Core computational metrics include: ① CAI (Codon Adaptation Index): measures how closely selected synonymous codons match abundant human tRNAs; ② GC%: the proportion of G and C nucleotides; ③ MFE (Minimum Free Energy, computed by tools like ViennaRNA, where more negative values denote tighter secondary structure—moderately low MFE in the CDS resists hydrolytic degradation, whereas excessive folding in the 5' UTR initiation region impedes ribosomal scanning and translation); ④ DegScore: predicts hydrolysis-prone cleavage sites along the RNA chain in aqueous solution; and ⑤ m1Ψ (N1-methylpseudouridine modification) & Type I Interferon (Type I IFN): chemical nucleoside modification prevents exogenous mRNA from over-activating innate immune interferon storms, thereby preventing translational shutdown.
Single-Cell Multi-Omics & Cancer Drug Resistance: scRNA-seq, CyTOF, Batch Effect, ecDNA, Polypharmacology & PROTAC
Systems & Oncology Pharmacology
Uncovering anticancer drug resistance mechanisms and next-generation drug modalities at single-cell resolution and dynamic genomic amplification scales.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
scRNA-seq (single-cell RNA sequencing) and CyTOF (mass cytometry) dissect the tumor microenvironment cell by cell (such as 4-1BB+ regulatory T cells [Tregs] driving resistance to PD-1/CTLA-4 immunotherapy), requiring rigorous quality control (QC) and Batch Effect correction prior to analysis. ecDNA (extrachromosomal circular DNA) segregates unequally and randomly during cancer cell division, causing oncogene copy numbers to surge within months and driving explosive targeted-therapy resistance. Polypharmacology uses drug-induced Differentially Expressed Genes (DEGs) to characterize network-wide phenotypic effects, whereas PROTACs are an emerging drug modality that hijacks the intracellular ubiquitin–proteasome system to directly degrade disease-causing proteins.

Frozen Feature Extraction (Frozen Embeddings) vs. Full Fine-Tuning & LoRA
Beginner-Friendly Equalizer
Locking a foundation model's original weights and using it purely as an "advanced feature extractor" lets you run top-journal baselines on free Google Colab in minutes.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
On small biomedical datasets of only a few thousand samples, modifying every parameter of a massive foundation model (Full Fine-Tuning) not only demands expensive GPU compute but also easily collapses from severe overfitting. The Frozen (Freeze) strategy keeps pre-trained weights fixed, directly extracts sequence vectors (applying Mean Pooling while masking out Padding Tokens to turn variable-length sequences into fixed-length vectors), and feeds them into lightweight LightGBM tree models or MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) classifiers; meanwhile, LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) trains only a tiny fraction of bypass adapter parameters for lightweight fine-tuning.
Anti-Leakage Data Splits: Homology Cluster Split & Bemis–Murcko Scaffold Split
#1 Code Review Red Line
Rejecting self-deceptive Random Splits by forcing the test set to contain only unseen sequence families or novel chemical core scaffolds.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
Under a naive random split, highly similar homologous peptides or "twin molecules" differing by just a single methyl group appear in both the training and test sets, artificially inflating accuracy (Data Leakage); likewise, performing global feature normalization (Scaling) prior to splitting leaks the test-set distribution. Rigorous practice requires: clustering peptides/epitopes by sequence similarity or holding out unseen MHC alleles (Held-out MHC); and for small molecules, calling RDKit to extract Bemis–Murcko ring scaffolds (Scaffold Split) so the test set consists entirely of novel chemical cores, genuinely testing generalization.
Imbalanced Biomedical Metrics: AUROC, AUPRC (Area Under Precision-Recall Curve), MCC (Matthews Correlation Coefficient), RMSE
Rigorous Statistical Evaluation
In real-world drug discovery datasets where "only 2 out of 100 molecules are active," AUPRC and MCC reveal a model's true discriminative power.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
Biological screening datasets typically exhibit extreme positive-to-negative class imbalance. Under such skew, plain accuracy or AUROC can easily appear deceptively high while top-selected candidates are flooded with false positives. AUPRC specifically measures hit purity (precision) and recall among high-confidence predictions, while MCC (ranging from -1 to +1) synthesizes all four cells of the confusion matrix (true/false positives and true/false negatives)—making it the hardcore classification metric most valued by top Korean bioinformatics journals; RMSE (Root Mean Square Error) is used for continuous numerical regression tasks (such as clearance prediction).
Interpretability & High-Dimensional Statistics: SHAP Attribution, LASSO Regularization, Curse of Dimensionality & FDR

Epitope, pMHC Presentation, Neoantigen & Antibody Fc Effector Functions (ADCC/ADCP)
Computational Immunology & Vaccines
Two computational levers in vaccines and immunotherapy: selecting short antigenic peptides upfront to prime T/B cells, and optimizing antibody clearance functions downstream.

An Epitope is the key short peptide fragment on a pathogen or tumor protein (tumor-specific mutant epitopes are termed Neoantigens) recognized by the immune system, which must bind and be presented by Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC-I/II) molecules (pMHC) to activate T cells. In antibody-mediated protection, beyond viral neutralization via the head Fab domain, binding of the tail Fc domain to immune-cell receptors to trigger Antibody-Dependent Cellular Cytotoxicity (ADCC) and Phagocytosis (ADCP) is equally critical—Systems Serology uses machine learning to integrate multidimensional antibody subclasses (e.g., IgG3) and glycosylation features to map vaccine protective immunity holistically.
Therapeutic Index / Safety Gating, Erythrocyte Hemolysis & Allergenicity
Peptide Safety Red Line
High antimicrobial or immunogenic potency alone is not enough—dangerous peptides that puncture human red blood cell membranes or trigger allergic reactions must be filtered out during in silico screening.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
Many potent antimicrobial or cationic peptides carry excessive positive net charge and contiguous hydrophobic amino acids, causing them to insert readily into human erythrocyte phospholipid bilayers and induce membrane pore formation and rupture (Hemolysis). Jointly modeling "immunogenicity / antimicrobial activity (e.g., Minimum Inhibitory Concentration, MIC)" alongside "hemolysis / cytotoxicity / allergenicity" in code to delineate a 2D high-activity, low-toxicity screening zone constitutes computational pharmacology's "Therapeutic Window safety gating."
ADMET, Hepatic Microsomal Stability (HLM/MLM), CYP450 Metabolic Soft Spots & hERG Cardiotoxicity
PK/Toxicology Core Expertise
Core pharmacokinetic and toxicological parameters determining whether a new drug candidate survives in animals and humans without causing liver or cardiac injury.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
ADMET stands for Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, and Toxicity. Hepatic Cytochrome P450 (CYP450) enzymes are the primary machinery breaking down xenobiotic drugs, and the vulnerable chemical bonds on a molecule most susceptible to CYP450 oxidation, dealkylation, or hydrolysis are called "Metabolic Soft Spots." Because mouse (MLM) and human liver microsomes (HLM) exhibit marked interspecies differences in CYP450 isoform composition and clearance kinetics—and translating in vitro assays to real in vivo exposure is heavily shaped by plasma protein binding—cross-species and in vitro-to-in vivo transfer modeling is exceptionally valuable; meanwhile, hERG is a critical cardiac potassium channel toxicity target that must be avoided to prevent arrhythmia.
mRNA Vaccine Sequence Engineering: 5'/3' UTR, CDS, CAI, GC%, Minimum Free Energy (MFE), DegScore & m1Ψ Modification
Nucleic Acid Vaccine Loop
The exact same antigen protein can be encoded by countless mRNA sequences; tuning codons, UTRs, and folding structure multiplies in vivo protein yield and stability.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
An mRNA construct consists of the antigen-encoding Coding Sequence (CDS) flanked by 5' and 3' Untranslated Regions (5' UTR and 3' UTR) that regulate ribosome binding and mRNA half-life. Core computational metrics include: ① CAI (Codon Adaptation Index): measures how closely selected synonymous codons match abundant human tRNAs; ② GC%: the proportion of G and C nucleotides; ③ MFE (Minimum Free Energy, computed by tools like ViennaRNA, where more negative values denote tighter secondary structure—moderately low MFE in the CDS resists hydrolytic degradation, whereas excessive folding in the 5' UTR initiation region impedes ribosomal scanning and translation); ④ DegScore: predicts hydrolysis-prone cleavage sites along the RNA chain in aqueous solution; and ⑤ m1Ψ (N1-methylpseudouridine modification) & Type I Interferon (Type I IFN): chemical nucleoside modification prevents exogenous mRNA from over-activating innate immune interferon storms, thereby preventing translational shutdown.
Single-Cell Multi-Omics & Cancer Drug Resistance: scRNA-seq, CyTOF, Batch Effect, ecDNA, Polypharmacology & PROTAC
Systems & Oncology Pharmacology
Uncovering anticancer drug resistance mechanisms and next-generation drug modalities at single-cell resolution and dynamic genomic amplification scales.
▾ Click to expand/collapse details
scRNA-seq (single-cell RNA sequencing) and CyTOF (mass cytometry) dissect the tumor microenvironment cell by cell (such as 4-1BB+ regulatory T cells [Tregs] driving resistance to PD-1/CTLA-4 immunotherapy), requiring rigorous quality control (QC) and Batch Effect correction prior to analysis. ecDNA (extrachromosomal circular DNA) segregates unequally and randomly during cancer cell division, causing oncogene copy numbers to surge within months and driving explosive targeted-therapy resistance.
