High-Score Breakthrough Strategy (Expert Notes)

"At the higher score bands, IELTS Writing is no longer purely a language test, but rather a 'logic and debate exam.' Many native English-speaking university students who take the test without preparation often only score a 6.5. This is because they think, 'My English is so good, I can just write whatever.' As a result, due to a lack of rigorous logic or going off-topic, their writing completely fails to meet the official IELTS requirements."

🎧 Listening Pushing for 7.5 - 8

Candidates scoring a 7 typically do not lose points in Part 1 and Part 2; their main stumbling blocks are Part 3 (multiple-choice for long conversations) and Part 4 (academic lecture fill-in-the-blanks).

He no longer needs extensive general listening. It is recommended that he specifically tackles Part 3 multiple-choice questions daily to build his anti-interference ability to "read long options while listening." Simultaneously, he should compile common synonym replacements for Part 4 fill-in-the-blank questions.

📖 Reading Pushing for 7.5 - 8

Scoring a 7 in Reading indicates that vocabulary and the parsing of complex sentences are absolutely not an issue. Any further point loss is often because the candidate gets tangled in the convoluted logic of True/False/Not Given questions, or misses the mark when summarizing paragraph main ideas (Heading questions).

Have him isolate these two specific question types from his past mistakes. He shouldn't focus on doing more new practice tests, but rather on purely understanding what the "trap logic" set by the examiners actually is.

✍️ Writing Strategy & Bottlenecks

  • High-Quality Grading (Most Important): Instead of watching videos and comparing essays on his own, it is much better to find a reliable IELTS teacher or use high-quality AI grading tools for detailed corrections. An external perspective is necessary to point out logical gaps and unnatural word choices.
  • Deep Revision > Frantic Writing: Do not blindly rush. Write 2 essays a week, but each one must go through a closed loop: "write -> get it graded -> summarize mistakes -> rewrite," until the logic and vocabulary reach the 7-band standard.
  • Core of Band 7: Task 2's Task Response (perfectly addresses prompt, has depth) and Coherence & Cohesion (argument flows smoothly). Requires extensive outlining practice, not padding word count.
  • ⚠️ Persian Native "Hidden Bottlenecks" 1) Invisible point loss from "Articles and Prepositions": Persian does not have a strict definite/indefinite article system like "a/an/the". Many native speakers frequently omit "the" or misuse prepositions. IELTS examiners hold a high standard for GRA (Grammar). If riddled with these flaws, the score locks at 6.5. 2) "Too Ornate but Lack Direct Logic": Persian is poetic, leading to long, roundabout sentences. IELTS Academic Task 2 logic is extremely direct and "utilitarian"—examiners want straightforward arguments and rigorous deduction. Sacrificing clarity for fancy syntax will backfire.

🗣️ Speaking Tactics: Reaction Speed

He can collect common topics, predict subjects for conversation practice, and improve reaction speed.

1. "Legally" Lie & Abandon Depth

In IELTS, "truth" is worthless; being "fluent" is king. Pick the simplest words and "make it up".

"No way! It sounds super boring to me. I'm an energetic person and I prefer working outdoors or interacting with people rather than standing next to old objects all day."

2. Buy Time with "Nonsense" (Fillers)

Memorize sets of native-like "buffering sentences".

"Well, to be honest, I've never really thought about it before, but off the top of my head I'd say..."

"That's quite an interesting question. If I had to choose, I suppose..."

3. The Timeline (PPF) Method

Surviving the "2-minute torture" using Past-Present-Future.

  • Past: First watched his movie years ago, thought it was okay...
  • Present: But recent work changed my view, acting is...
  • Future: I heard he'll direct a new movie next year, I'll definitely go to the cinema...
